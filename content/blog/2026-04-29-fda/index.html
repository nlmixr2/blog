nlmixr2 Meets with FDA: Key Signals and What’s Next

We recently met with the FDA to discuss nlmixr2 and what helps open, community-built modeling tools succeed in regulatory settings. The conversation was constructive and forward-looking, with a clear theme: make it easier for reviewers to trust, reproduce, and exchange models across tools.

As we go through these reflections, I must add that these are my reflections and are not necessarily the views of the FDA nor the views of the companies or individuals represented by the nlmixr2 working group or the R consortium.

Highlights from the discussion

Tool-agnostic approach: The FDA emphasized they are tool agnostic in submissions and have accepted nlmixr2-based submissions (as well as many other tools). Since they are tool agnostic, they really resonated with conversion between modeling tools.

For this conversion, I recall the following points:

  • Strong interest in model exchange: They were excited about model interchange and what it could unlock for reviewers and sponsors.

  • Export matters for review: While discussing monolix2rx we noted that their excellent model library could be imported into nlmixr2 when the model text-file was included in the project (an opt-in option), or if they have a Monolix license to import the model from the Monolix model library. There was some discussion about how having the fully exported model from Monolix is desirable in submission (and would be in any other tool). This full model specification would make for more consistent reviews, especially when the tools help with the exchange.

  • Integration request: They expressed interest in the eventual ability to exchange between any model format (ie Pumas, Phoenix, etc). While we at the nlmixr2 team are not looking to translate to other formats currently, we understand the gap for system exchange and would love to have this available in the open source world as well.

Why this matters

The direction is encouraging: interoperability, transparency, and reviewer-friendly workflows are important. Our focus moving forward is to strengthen regulatory readiness through clear validation signals, smooth model exchange, and better training resources.

FAQ — Regulatory Readiness and Model Exchange

What does “regulatory readiness” mean for nlmixr2?

It means the tool and workflows support reproducible results, clear documentation, and reviewer-friendly outputs, so regulators can confidently follow what was done and why—without ambiguity.

Has nlmixr2 been used in regulatory submissions?

Yes. The FDA indicated they have accepted submissions that used nlmixr2

Does the FDA require a specific modeling tool?

No. A key point shared was that the FDA is tool agnostic—the emphasis is on scientific validity, clarity, and reproducibility, not the brand of software.

What is “model exchange” (or model interchange), and why is it important?

Model exchange is the ability to move a model between tools without losing meaning or correctness. It matters because it can:

  • Make reviews more consistent

  • Reduce translation errors

  • Help different reviewers work in the tools they know best

What did the FDA say about model exchange specifically?

They were excited about model interchange and emphasized the importance of full model export, particularly from Monolix, to support review workflows without depending on external model libraries.

Why does “full model export” matter?

Because reviewers need to see the complete, self-contained model definition. Relying on separate libraries or implicit dependencies can make it harder to validate what was actually run and compared, especially if the reviewer isn’t as familiar with the tool.
